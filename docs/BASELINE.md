# BASELINE.md

The XY-only baseline for the outcome of an on-ball engagement. Phase 3 of docs/PLAN.md.
Design fixed in docs/PREREGISTRATION.md before this was run. Status: done on 10 November 2026.

The baseline answers one question: how well can the primary outcome (the pressed possession ends in a possession loss)
be predicted from XY tracking and event context at the start of the engagement, without body pose?
It is fitted on the 18 matches without pose and then frozen. Phase 4 asks what pose adds on top of it.

No pose feature was computed for this document. The orientation tables were read for their keys only (which player has
joints in which frame), to apply inclusion criterion 1.

### 1.1 Sources and definitions

- Design: docs/PREREGISTRATION.md sections 3, 5 and 7 to 9. All numbers in this block are produced by `scripts/xy_baseline.py`.
- Primary outcome: possession loss of the pressed possession (`associated_player_possession_end_type == possession_loss`).
- Features: 14 numeric and 7 categorical XY features (`blindside.features.xy_baseline.BASELINE_FEATURES`). No pose feature.
- Model: L2 logistic regression, `C` chosen by grouped 5-fold cross-validation over the training matches, then frozen.
- Intervals: cluster bootstrap over pressed possessions, 2,000 resamples, stratified by match, percentile 95%. Differences are paired.

## 2. Reading of the results

Measured:
- **The test set is large enough for the pre-registered test.** 1,875 engagements in 1,382 possessions pass all three inclusion
  criteria, with 217 possession losses (11.6%). The pre-registered minimum of 100 events is met, so Phase 4 runs as an incremental
  value test and the cut rule of docs/PLAN.md does not apply.
- **The baseline transfers to the pose matches.** Log loss 0.310 against 0.361 for the constant prediction, difference
  minus 0.050 (95% interval minus 0.071 to minus 0.030). ROC AUC 0.76 (0.72 to 0.80). It beats the constant in each pose match
  separately, with the interval of the difference below zero in both.
- **The pose matches are ordinary matches for this model.** Leave-one-match-out on the 18 training matches gives a mean log loss
  of 0.336 (range 0.281 to 0.404) and a mean AUC of 0.78 (0.73 to 0.82). The pose matches score 0.318 and 0.303, AUC 0.80 and 0.73:
  inside the range of the training matches.
- **The baseline is somewhat overconfident on the pose matches.** Calibration intercept minus 0.25, slope 0.89. The loss rate
  is lower there (11.6% against 13.8%). This is why Phase 4 compares against a recalibrated baseline (Model 0), not the raw one.
- **Match-to-match spread is larger than the gain from any single feature group.** The log loss of the constant prediction alone
  ranges from 0.33 to 0.50 across matches, because the loss rate ranges from 10% to 19%.
- **The SkillCorner labels carry part of the signal.** Without them the log loss is 0.317 instead of 0.310. The pressure label has
  the largest coefficients (very high pressure: odds ratio 3.4, no pressure: 0.31).

What this does not show:
- Nothing about pose. A strong XY baseline makes an increment harder to find. It does not make one less likely to exist.
- The coefficients are not effects. Features are correlated and penalised.

Caveats, labelled:
- **Timing of the SkillCorner labels (known when pre-registering).** `overall_pressure_start` and `reception_difficulty_start`
  are measured at possession start, which is after the engagement start for a majority of engagements. They are in the baseline
  as pre-registered, and the variant without them is reported above and will be repeated in Phase 4.
- **Phase type (found after pre-registering).** `team_out_of_possession_phase_type` has large coefficients for the levels
  `chaotic` and `disruption`. Whether SkillCorner assigns a phase type using what happens later in the phase is not documented
  in the open data. If it does, this feature leaks outcome information into the baseline. That would favour the baseline and
  make the test for pose harder, so the pre-registered feature set stays as it is. Recorded in docs/PREREGISTRATION.md section 12.
- Two pose matches. The intervals describe sampling of possessions inside these matches, not of matches.
